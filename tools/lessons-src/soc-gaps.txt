unit: Biomes of the world | 7 | soc_7_n1

lesson: What makes a biome
mc1: What is a biome? | a large region with a similar climate, plants and animals | a single pond or patch of woods | the land inside a country's borders | a layer of the atmosphere
hint: Think big: a biome can stretch across several continents.
mc1: Which two climate factors do the most to decide which biome forms in a place? | temperature and precipitation | elevation and population | wind speed and soil color | latitude and time zone
hint: Climate graphs usually show two things for each month. What are they?
mc1: Which treeless biome near the Arctic has ground that stays frozen all year just below the surface, called permafrost? | tundra | taiga | desert | savanna
hint: Summers there are short and cool, and only low plants such as mosses and lichens grow.
mc1: Which biome has grasses with scattered trees and a long dry season, as in much of East Africa? | savanna | tropical rainforest | taiga | temperate deciduous forest
hint: Think of giraffes eating from lone acacia trees on open plains.
mc1: Which biome is a huge forest of cone-bearing trees such as spruce and fir that stretches across Canada and Russia? | taiga | tropical rainforest | temperate deciduous forest | savanna
hint: This biome is also called the boreal forest, which means "northern."
mc1: Which biome can be hot or cold but always gets very little precipitation? | desert | tropical rainforest | temperate deciduous forest | savanna
hint: The Sahara is hot and the Gobi has freezing winters, yet both belong to this biome.

lesson: Reading climate data
mc2: A place averages about 27 C every month and gets about 250 cm of rain a year. Which biome is it? | tropical rainforest | savanna | desert | temperate deciduous forest
hint: It is warm all year and very wet. Where on Earth is that true?
mc2: A place has winters near -30 C, a short summer that reaches about 10 C, about 20 cm of precipitation a year and no trees. Which biome is it? | tundra | taiga | desert | grassland
hint: Trees cannot grow there. Is that because of dryness or because of cold?
mc2: A place has summers near 25 C, winters near 0 C and about 120 cm of precipitation spread across the year. Its trees drop their leaves each fall. Which biome is it? | temperate deciduous forest | taiga | tropical rainforest | grassland
hint: Four clear seasons and broad leaves that change color point to one biome.
mc2: A place averages 24 C all year. It gets 90 cm of rain, almost all of it in five wet months, and has grasses with scattered trees. Which biome is it? | savanna | tropical rainforest | desert | temperate deciduous forest
hint: Warm all year, but with a long dry season. A rainforest would be wet every month.
mc2: A place has hot summers, cold winters, about 40 cm of precipitation a year, deep fertile soil and few trees. Which biome is it? | grassland | desert | taiga | tropical rainforest
hint: There is enough rain for tall grass but not enough for a forest.
mc2: A place has winters averaging -20 C, summers averaging 15 C and about 50 cm of precipitation a year. Thick forests of evergreen needle-leaf trees cover it. Which biome is it? | taiga | tundra | temperate deciduous forest | grassland
hint: The tundra has no trees, and deciduous trees have broad leaves.

lesson: How people live in each biome
mc2: In the Arctic tundra, why are many buildings raised on posts above the ground? | so heat from the building does not thaw the permafrost beneath it | to stay above floods from heavy rain | to rise above tall grass | to catch cooling sea breezes
hint: What would happen to frozen ground if a warm house sat right on it?
mc2: Many herders on the grasslands of Mongolia live in round felt tents called gers that can be taken down in a few hours. Why does this design fit their way of life? | they move their herds to fresh pastures with the seasons | the grassland has many trees for building | they stay in one place all year | the climate is hot all year
hint: Herd animals eat the grass in one area. What must the herders do next?
mc2: Traditional homes in hot deserts often have thick mud-brick walls and small windows. Why? | thick walls slow the heat by day and release it at night, and small windows let in less sun | thick walls keep out heavy rain | the walls trap snow to keep the house cool | mud bricks are the only material sold in cities
hint: Deserts are often very hot by day and cool at night.
mc2: In tropical rainforests, many traditional houses are built on stilts with steep roofs. What is the main reason? | to stay dry during floods and shed heavy rain | to keep from thawing permafrost | to keep snow from piling up | to stay warm in cold winters
hint: Think about how much rain falls there each year.
mc2: In dry regions, how do many farmers get enough water to grow crops? | by irrigating from rivers, wells or oases | by melting permafrost | by relying on daily rainfall | by collecting snow from glaciers in their fields
hint: Rain is scarce, so water must be brought to the fields from somewhere else.
mc2: Many herders in the savanna move their cattle during the dry season. Why? | grass and water become scarce in the dry season, so they move to find more | the cattle need snow to drink | the wet season burns the grass | the ground freezes in the dry season
hint: What happens to plants and water holes when months pass with almost no rain?

lesson: Clearing up biome mix-ups
mc2: A student says all deserts are hot. Which fact shows the student is wrong? | the Gobi Desert has freezing winters | the Sahara is hot in summer | rainforests get a lot of rain | the tundra has permafrost
hint: A desert is defined by how little precipitation it gets, not by its temperature.
mc2: A student says the tundra must get a lot of precipitation because it is covered in snow much of the year. What is actually true? | the tundra gets little precipitation; snow lasts because it is too cold to melt for most of the year | the tundra gets more precipitation than any other biome | snow covers the tundra all year, so no plants ever grow | snow falls on the tundra only in summer
hint: Compare how much snow falls with how long it stays on the ground.
mc2: Why do tropical rainforests often have poor soil even though they have so many plants? | heavy rain washes nutrients away, and living plants quickly take up what is left | the soil there is frozen | very little rain falls there | there are no decomposers there
hint: Think about what heavy rain does to soil, and where most nutrients are stored in this biome.
mc2: What is the main difference between a savanna and a temperate grassland? | a savanna is warm all year with wet and dry seasons; a temperate grassland has hot summers and cold winters | a savanna has no grass at all | a temperate grassland lies on the equator | a savanna gets heavy snow every winter
hint: Compare where each is located: near the equator or in the middle latitudes?
mc2: What is the biggest cause of tropical rainforest loss today? | clearing forest for farms, cattle ranches and plantations | ocean pollution | spreading tundra | volcanic eruptions
hint: Think about why people cut down large areas of forest.
mc2: Why does the taiga have mostly evergreen conifers instead of broadleaf trees? | needles lose little water and trees can make food as soon as it warms, without growing new leaves | the taiga is hot and wet all year | conifers need more rain than broadleaf trees | the soil is too rich for broadleaf trees
hint: The growing season is very short. What advantage is there in keeping leaves all year?

lesson: Biomes and real decisions
mc3: Engineers plan a new road across the tundra in Alaska. Which problem must they plan for most carefully? | heat from the road could thaw the permafrost and make the ground sink | heavy monsoon rains | sand dunes drifting over the road | thick rainforest vines
hint: Think about what lies just under the surface in this biome.
mc3: A town gets an average of 15 cm of precipitation a year, and summer highs are often above 40 C. Planners want to add many grass lawns and golf courses. What is the strongest concern? | water is scarce, so lawns would use water needed for drinking and farming | the lawns would freeze every winter | heavy rain would flood the lawns | tall trees would shade the lawns
hint: Use the climate numbers to identify the biome first.
num2: A place gets 2 cm of precipitation in each of its 8 driest months and 3 cm in each of its other 4 months. What is its total precipitation for the year, in centimeters? | 28
hint: Find the total for the dry months and the total for the other months, then add.
mc3: A place stays between 25 C and 30 C all year. From May through October it gets about 20 cm of rain each month, and from November through April only about 1 cm each month. Grasses cover the land with scattered trees. Which biome is it? | savanna | tropical rainforest | desert | temperate deciduous forest
hint: It is warm all year, but half the year is very dry. Which biome has that pattern?
mc3: Warmer temperatures are letting trees and shrubs grow farther north than before. Which change would you expect at the edge of the tundra? | shrubs and taiga trees spreading into areas that were treeless | more permafrost forming each year | the tundra turning into tropical rainforest | the growing season getting shorter
hint: The tundra and the taiga meet at a boundary called the tree line. Which way would it move?
sa3: Choose one biome. Explain two ways people there have adapted to its climate and one way people have changed its environment. | Desert: people build thick-walled homes with small windows to stay cool, and they wear loose, light clothing that covers the skin. They have changed the environment by pumping groundwater and building canals for irrigation, which can lower water tables.

unit: The social sciences | 13 | soc_13_n1

lesson: The fields of social science
mc1: Which social science studies how people, businesses and governments make choices about using scarce resources? | economics | sociology | psychology | geography
hint: Think of the idea that wants are unlimited but resources are limited.
mc1: Which field studies individual mental processes and behavior, such as memory, emotion and learning? | psychology | sociology | anthropology | political science
hint: Its focus is the mind of the individual person.
mc1: Which field studies social groups, institutions and patterns within societies, such as family, social class and religion? | sociology | psychology | economics | history
hint: This field looks at how groups shape people, not at one person's mind.
mc1: Which field studies human cultures and human origins, often through long-term fieldwork inside a community? | anthropology | economics | political science | psychology
hint: Its name comes from the Greek word for "human being."
mc1: Which field studies where people, places and environments are found on Earth's surface and how they interact? | geography | history | economics | psychology
hint: Maps and the question "Why here?" are central to this field.
mc1: Which field studies governments, power, elections and public policy? | political science | anthropology | psychology | geography
hint: Think of the systems that make and enforce rules for a society.

lesson: Which field asks this question?
mc2: Which social science is the best fit for this question: "How does a rise in the minimum wage affect the number of teenagers who have jobs?" | economics | anthropology | geography | psychology
hint: The question is about wages, jobs and the choices employers make.
mc2: Which social science is the best fit for this question: "How do the locations of rivers and mountains shape where cities grow?" | geography | psychology | political science | sociology
hint: The question is about places and physical features.
mc2: Which social science is the best fit for this question: "How does losing sleep affect a person's short-term memory?" | psychology | economics | sociology | history
hint: Is the question about a group or about one person's mind?
mc2: Which social science is the best fit for this question: "What caused the Western Roman Empire to collapse in the 400s CE?" | history | psychology | sociology | geography
hint: The question asks about causes of events long ago.
mc2: Which social science is the best fit for this question: "How do voters' party loyalties affect which candidates win close national elections?" | political science | anthropology | geography | history
hint: Elections and political parties are the heart of the question.
mc2: Which social science is the best fit for this question: "How do marriage customs and family ties differ between two farming villages, studied by living in each village for a year?" | anthropology | economics | political science | psychology
hint: Living inside a community to understand its culture is a method of one field.

lesson: How social scientists find answers
mc2: A researcher lives in a community for a year, takes part in daily life and keeps detailed field notes. What is this method called? | participant observation | a controlled experiment | a census | content analysis
hint: The researcher both watches and joins in.
mc2: A psychologist randomly assigns students to sleep either 4 hours or 8 hours, then gives everyone the same memory test. What method is this? | a controlled experiment | a case study | a survey | an ethnography
hint: The researcher changes one factor on purpose and compares the groups.
mc2: A sociologist asks 2,000 randomly chosen adults the same set of questions about how much they trust their neighbors. What method is this? | a survey | an experiment | participant observation | primary-source analysis
hint: The researcher does not change anything; the data are people's answers to questions.
mc2: When a historian judges how reliable a source is, which question matters most? | who created it, when, and for what purpose | whether it agrees with the historian's own view | whether it was written recently | whether it is a famous document
hint: Every source was made by someone with a point of view and a reason.
mc2: A geographer uses GIS software to layer maps of flood zones, household income and hospital locations. What is the main advantage of this method? | it shows spatial patterns and relationships among different kinds of data | it proves that one factor causes another | it replaces all fieldwork | it lets the geographer interview residents
hint: Think about what you can see when maps are stacked on top of each other.
mc3: An economist finds that cities with more coffee shops have higher average incomes. Why can she not conclude that coffee shops cause higher incomes? | the data show a correlation; a third factor or reverse causation could explain it | her sample of cities is too large | economists are not allowed to study cities | coffee shops are a primary source
hint: Could higher incomes lead to more coffee shops, instead of the other way around?

lesson: Common mix-ups in the social sciences
mc2: Which statement best separates sociology from psychology? | sociology focuses on groups and social patterns; psychology focuses on individual minds and behavior | sociology uses only experiments; psychology uses only surveys | psychology studies the past; sociology studies the present | the two fields study exactly the same things
hint: Think about the level each field looks at: the group or the person.
mc2: What is the difference between a primary source and a secondary source in history? | a primary source was made at the time by a participant or witness; a secondary source interprets events later | a primary source is always more accurate than a secondary source | a secondary source is always older than a primary source | a primary source is always a government document
hint: A diary from 1863 and a textbook chapter about 1863 are two different kinds of sources.
mc2: A student says economics is only about money and the stock market. Which is a better description? | economics studies how people, firms and governments choose to use scarce resources, including time and labor | economics studies only banks and interest rates | economics studies only government budgets | economics studies only how prices are printed on goods
hint: Deciding how to spend an hour is also an economic choice.
mc2: Which pair correctly matches a branch of a social science with what it studies? | microeconomics: the choices of individual households and firms | cultural anthropology: the fossils of early humans | political theory: the shapes of landforms | cognitive psychology: the results of national elections
hint: Test each pair: would a scholar in that branch really study that topic?
mc2: A researcher studies how glaciers carved river valleys over thousands of years. Which branch of geography is this? | physical geography | human geography | cultural geography | political geography
hint: Is the researcher studying people or natural features?
mc2: Why do social scientists use random samples in surveys? | so the sample represents the population and results can be generalized | so researchers can choose people likely to agree | so the survey costs nothing | because random samples guarantee there is no error
hint: A random sample reduces bias, but can it remove all error?

lesson: Social science in the real world
mc3: A city wants to know why fewer young adults vote than older adults. Which pairing of field and method fits best? | political science, using a survey that asks voters and nonvoters about their reasons | history, using only documents from ancient Rome | psychology, using fossil records | geography, using rock samples from the city
hint: Match the question to a field, then ask which method could actually collect people's reasons.
mc3: A team studies how a new highway changed a rural town. Which question is the most clearly geographic? | How did the highway change where businesses and homes were located? | How did residents feel about the changes? | How did the town council vote on the highway? | What did the town look like in 1900?
hint: Geography asks about location and the use of space.
mc3: A city starts a free bus program. Which question would an economist most likely ask about it? | How do riders' savings in money and time compare with the program's cost to the city? | How did bus drivers' families celebrate holidays? | What did the city's first buses look like? | Which bus routes appear on old maps?
hint: Economists compare costs and benefits.
mc3: A study finds that students who eat breakfast earn higher grades. Which follow-up study best tests whether breakfast causes better grades? | randomly assign some students to get a free breakfast and compare their grades with similar students who do not | survey more students about what they eat | ask teachers whether they think breakfast matters | compare grades of students in two different countries
hint: To test cause, the researcher must control who gets breakfast.
mc3: An anthropologist judges a community's customs as strange and wrong simply because they differ from her own. Which concept describes this bias? | ethnocentrism | cultural relativism | assimilation | globalization
hint: Cultural relativism is the opposite attitude: judging customs within their own culture.
sa3: Choose a social issue, such as rising housing costs. Describe how two different social sciences would study it, naming a question and a method for each. | Economics would ask how supply and demand set housing prices, using data on prices, wages and new construction. Sociology would ask how rising rents change neighborhoods and families, using surveys and interviews with residents who stayed or moved.
