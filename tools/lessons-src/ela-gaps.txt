lesson: Academic words you will meet often
mc1: "The committee's report was so concise that it fit on a single page without leaving out anything important." What does "concise" mean? | brief but complete | confusing and unclear | long and wordy | angry in tone

mc1: "The new evidence did not weaken the theory; in fact, it corroborated the main claim." What does "corroborated" mean? | confirmed or supported | contradicted | ignored | invented
hint: "In fact" signals the opposite of "did not weaken."
mc1: "Arjun was ambivalent about the job offer: he loved the city but dreaded the long hours." What does "ambivalent" mean? | having mixed feelings | not interested at all | very excited | openly angry
hint: Look at the two halves after the colon. Do they point the same way?
mc1: "The engineer was meticulous, checking every measurement three times before signing off." What does "meticulous" mean? | extremely careful about details | careless | quick and hurried | secretive
hint: What kind of person checks every number three times?
mc1: "The long drought had a detrimental effect on the harvest, cutting it nearly in half." What does "detrimental" mean? | harmful | helpful | slight | sudden
hint: A harvest cut nearly in half is a bad result.
mc1: "Although Ines was usually reticent in meetings, she spoke at length about her research." What does "reticent" mean? | reserved and slow to speak | talkative | angry | uninformed
hint: "Although" signals a contrast with speaking at length.

lesson: Reading meaning from context
mc2: "Unlike her gregarious brother, who chatted with everyone at the party, Mei preferred a quiet talk with one friend." What does "gregarious" mean? | sociable and outgoing | shy and quiet | rude | forgetful
hint: "Unlike" signals that the brother is the opposite of Mei.
mc2: "The lecture was so esoteric that only the three specialists in the room could follow it." What does "esoteric" mean? | understood by only a small group of experts | simple and clear | dull and boring | loud
hint: Who could follow the lecture, and who could not?
mc2: "The mayor's plan was feasible: the city had the money, the workers and the land to carry it out." What does "feasible" mean? | possible to do | very expensive | unpopular | already finished
hint: The details after the colon list what the city already had.
mc2: "The essay was replete with examples; nearly every paragraph offered two or three." What does "replete" mean? | filled | lacking | free | tired
hint: Two or three examples in nearly every paragraph is a lot.
mc2: "Tomas tried to mitigate the damage from the leak by setting buckets under the drips and moving the books to a dry room." What does "mitigate" mean? | lessen | cause | ignore | measure
hint: Buckets and moving books do not stop the leak. What do they do to the damage?
mc2: "Online fame is often ephemeral; a video everyone shares on Monday may be forgotten by Friday." What does "ephemeral" mean? | short-lived | permanent | widely known | harmful
hint: How long did the video's fame last?

lesson: Roots and affixes
mc2: The root "bene" means "good," and "vol" means "wish." What does "benevolent" most likely mean? | kind and wanting to help others | violent and cruel | very wealthy | forgetful
hint: Put the two root meanings together.
mc2: The prefix "mal-" means "bad." In the sentence "A rival tried to malign the candidate by spreading false stories," what does "malign" mean? | to speak harmful untruths about | to support publicly | to ignore | to imitate
hint: What were the false stories meant to do to the candidate?
mc2: The root "chron" means "time." Which word means "arranged in the order in which events happened"? | chronological | chronic | synchronized | anachronism
hint: "Chronic" means lasting a long time, and "syn-" means together. Which word is about order?
mc2: The prefix "circum-" means "around." What does "circumvent" mean? | to find a way around a rule or obstacle | to travel in a full circle | to describe in great detail | to break into pieces
hint: The root "vent" means "come." Coming around something lets you avoid it.
mc2: The root "loqu" means "speak." What does "loquacious" mean? | very talkative | silent | logical | wealthy
hint: Ask how much a loquacious person speaks.
mc2: "The DNA results were incontrovertible; even the defense lawyer did not question them." Using in- (not), contro- (against) and vert (turn), what does "incontrovertible" mean? | impossible to dispute | doubtful | hard to obtain | very expensive
hint: Something that cannot be turned against or argued with is what?

lesson: Connotation and the precise word
mc2: Which word has the most positive connotation for a person who spends money carefully? | thrifty | stingy | cheap | miserly
hint: Three of the words suggest the person is unwilling to share or spend even when they should.
mc2: Which word for a person who holds firmly to an opinion has a negative connotation? | obstinate | determined | steadfast | resolute
hint: Three of the words praise the person. One suggests stubbornness.
mc2: Choose the most precise word: "The witness's account was ___: every detail matched the security video." | accurate | plausible | vivid | lengthy
hint: "Plausible" means believable, not proven. What does matching the video show?
mc2: Choose the best word: "The new medicine did not cure the disease, but it did ___ the symptoms." | alleviate | aggravate | diagnose | ignore
hint: The sentence says "but," so the medicine still helped in some way.
mc2: Choose the correct word: "From her yawning and heavy eyelids, I ___ that she was tired." | inferred | implied | insinuated | intimated
hint: The speaker draws a conclusion from clues. The person sending a hint does something else.
mc2: Choose the correct word: "A judge must be ___, with no personal stake in the outcome of the case." | disinterested | uninterested | bored | interested
hint: One of these words means "not caring," which a judge should never be. Another means "impartial."

lesson: Vocabulary in academic writing
mc3: A student writes, "The data shows that the medicine is good." Which revision uses the most precise academic language? | The data indicate that the medicine reduces symptoms in most patients. | The data shows that the medicine is really, really good. | The data proves the medicine is awesome. | It is obvious to everyone that the medicine is good.
hint: Precise writing says exactly what the medicine does and avoids slang and exaggeration.
mc3: "Because the findings were preliminary, the researchers warned readers not to draw firm conclusions yet." What does "preliminary" mean? | early, coming before a fuller study | final and complete | surprising | dangerous
hint: Why would researchers warn against firm conclusions?
mc3: "The historian's argument was cogent; each claim rested on documents and led logically to the next." Which word could replace "cogent" with the least change in meaning? | convincing | confusing | lengthy | emotional
hint: Claims backed by documents and logical steps make what kind of argument?
mc3: "'Oh, wonderful,' Keanu said in a sardonic tone, as the rain soaked his new shoes." What does "sardonic" mean? | mocking in a bitter way | cheerful | deeply sad | confused
hint: Is getting new shoes soaked really wonderful? What does that say about his tone?
mc3: Which word means "to make something seem less important than it really is"? | trivialize | exaggerate | emphasize | scrutinize
hint: Two of these words make something seem bigger. Of the other two, which one is about importance?

unit: Grammar and usage for college tests | 13 | ela_13_n1

lesson: Commonly confused words
mc1: Choose the correct word: "The company announced that ___ profits rose last year." | its | it's | its' | it
hint: You need a word that shows ownership. Does "it is" make sense here?
mc1: Choose the correct word: "___ been raining since noon." | It's | Its | Its' | Is
hint: Try reading the sentence with "It has" in the blank.
mc1: Choose the correct word: "The students left ___ backpacks in the hallway." | their | there | they're | there's
hint: The backpacks belong to the students.
mc1: Choose the correct word: "___ going to announce the results tomorrow." | They're | Their | There | Theirs
hint: Try "They are" in the blank.
mc1: Choose the correct word: "Is ___ any reason to delay the vote?" | there | their | they're | theirs
hint: The word points to whether something exists, not to who owns it.
mc1: Choose the correct word: "___ turn is it to present?" | Whose | Who's | Who | Whom
hint: Try "Who is" in the blank. Does it make sense?

lesson: Subject-verb agreement
mc2: Choose the correct verb: "The box of old letters ___ on the top shelf." | sits | sit | are sitting | have sat
hint: Cross out "of old letters." What is the subject?
mc2: Choose the correct verb: "Neither the coach nor the players ___ happy with the new schedule." | are | is | was | has been
hint: With "neither...nor," the verb agrees with the subject closer to it.
mc2: Choose the correct verb: "Each of the reports ___ a summary at the end." | includes | include | are including | have included
hint: "Each" means each one.
mc2: Choose the correct verb: "The list of required documents ___ posted online." | is | are | were | have been
hint: Cross out the phrase "of required documents" and find the subject.
mc2: Choose the correct verb: "There ___ several reasons for the delay." | are | is | was | has been
hint: In a "there" sentence, the subject comes after the verb. Find it.
mc2: Choose the correct verb: "The team, along with its two coaches, ___ traveling to the finals." | is | are | were | have been
hint: Words set off by "along with" do not change the number of the subject.

lesson: Pronouns: case, clarity and agreement

mc2: Choose the correct pronoun: "___ and her sister opened a bakery last spring." | She | Her | Herself | Hers

mc2: Choose the correct pronoun: "To ___ should the application be sent?" | whom | who | whose | who's
hint: Answer the question with "him" or "he." Which fits after "to"?
mc2: "When Lena met Rosa, she was nervous." Which revision fixes the unclear pronoun? | When Lena met Rosa, Lena was nervous. | When Lena met Rosa, she was very nervous. | She was nervous when Lena met Rosa. | When she met Rosa, she was nervous.
hint: In the original, "she" could mean either woman. Which choice leaves no doubt?
mc2: Choose the correct pronoun: "One of the bridges had ___ two lanes closed for repairs." | its | their | it's | there

mc2: Choose the correct pronoun: "The museum sent a letter thanking Dev and ___ for volunteering." | us | we | ourselves | our

lesson: Sentence boundaries and parallel structure
mc2: Which sentence is punctuated correctly? | The lab closed early; the power had failed. | The lab closed early, the power had failed. | The lab closed early the power had failed. | The lab closed early; because the power had failed.
hint: A semicolon must have a complete sentence on each side.
mc2: Which sentence is a comma splice? | The train was late, we missed the opening speech. | The train was late, so we missed the opening speech. | Because the train was late, we missed the opening speech. | The train was late; we missed the opening speech.
hint: A comma splice joins two complete sentences with only a comma.
mc2: Which revision corrects this run-on sentence: "Rain fell all night the roads flooded by morning"? | Rain fell all night, and the roads flooded by morning. | Rain fell all night, the roads flooded by morning. | Rain fell all night and, the roads flooded by morning. | Rain fell all night the roads, flooded by morning.
hint: Two complete sentences need a period, a semicolon, or a comma with a joining word such as "and."
mc2: Choose the answer that keeps the sentence parallel: "The internship taught me to write reports, to manage budgets, and ___." | to lead meetings | leading meetings | how meetings are led | the leading of meetings
hint: Look at the form of the first two items in the list.
mc2: Choose the answer that keeps the sentence parallel: "On weekends, Ade enjoys hiking, swimming, and ___." | biking | to bike | he bikes | a bike ride
hint: The first two items end in -ing.
mc2: Which sentence has parallel structure? | The plan is cheap, quick, and safe. | The plan is cheap, quick, and it is safe. | The plan is cheap, quickly, and safe. | The plan is cheap, quick, and has safety.
hint: Every item in the list should be the same kind of word.

lesson: Modifiers, apostrophes and mixed editing
mc2: "Walking to class, the rain soaked my notes." Which revision fixes the dangling modifier? | As I was walking to class, the rain soaked my notes. | Walking to class, my notes were soaked by the rain. | The rain soaked my notes, walking to class. | Walking to class, the rain soaked the notes I had.
hint: The rain was not walking to class. Which version says who was walking?
mc2: Choose the correct form: "The ___ locker room was repainted over the summer." (The locker room is shared by many girls.) | girls' | girl's | girls | girls's
hint: Make the plural first, then show ownership.
mc2: Choose the correct form: "The ___ new policy affects every employee." (There is one company.) | company's | companies | companys' | companies'
hint: One owner: add an apostrophe and s to the singular word.
mc2: Jamal and Kiri each own a separate car. Which phrase correctly completes "Both ___ cars were towed"? | Jamal's and Kiri's | Jamal and Kiri's | Jamals and Kiris | Jamal's and Kiris'
hint: When two people own things separately, each name shows ownership.
mc3: Which sentence has no errors? | The results of the survey are clear: most students want later start times. | The results of the survey is clear: most students want later start times. | The results of the survey are clear, most students want later start times. | The results of the survey are clear; most student's want later start times.
hint: Check subject-verb agreement, the punctuation between the two clauses, and every apostrophe.
mc3: Which revision corrects every error in "The scientists published there findings, they're conclusions surprised many"? | The scientists published their findings; their conclusions surprised many. | The scientists published their findings, their conclusions surprised many. | The scientists published there findings; their conclusions surprised many. | The scientists published their findings; they're conclusions surprised many.
hint: Look for three problems: two confused words and one comma splice.
