num1: A duck laid 4 eggs. 3 eggs hatched. How many eggs have not hatched yet? | 1

lesson: Sorting tricky animals

mc1: A bat can fly. It has fur and drinks milk as a baby. What group is a bat in? | mammal | bird | insect | reptile

mc1: A penguin cannot fly, but it has feathers and lays eggs. What group is a penguin in? | bird | fish | mammal | reptile
hint: Which group has feathers?
mc1: Which animal is an insect? | ant | spider | worm | snail

mc1: Which animal does NOT belong: robin, eagle, owl or bat? | bat | robin | eagle | owl
hint: Three of these animals have feathers.
mc1: A salamander has wet skin and lays eggs in water. What group is it in? | amphibian | reptile | fish | insect
hint: A salamander looks like a lizard, but its skin is different. Lizards have dry scales.

unit: Classifying living things | 6 | sci_6_n1

lesson: Why and how scientists classify
mc1: Why do scientists classify living things? | to sort them into groups by shared traits and show how they are related | to rank which organisms are most important | to give each organism a different common name in every language | to decide which organisms are dangerous to people
hint: Think about how a library groups books so similar ones are easy to find.
mc1: Which list shows the levels of classification from broadest to most specific? | domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species | species, genus, family, order, class, phylum, kingdom, domain | kingdom, domain, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species | domain, kingdom, class, phylum, order, family, species, genus
hint: Start with the level that holds the most kinds of organisms and end with the level that holds just one kind.
mc1: Which scientist created the two-part naming system for species that is still used today? | Carl Linnaeus | Charles Darwin | Gregor Mendel | Robert Hooke
hint: He was a Swedish botanist in the 1700s who named thousands of plants and animals.
mc1: As you move from the kingdom level down to the species level, what happens to the organisms in each group? | there are fewer kinds, and they are more alike | there are more kinds, and they are more alike | there are fewer kinds, and they are less alike | the number of kinds stays the same
hint: Compare all animals with only the cats.
mc2: Two organisms are in the same genus. Which other level must they also share? | family | species | subspecies | no other level besides genus
hint: The levels nest inside each other like boxes inside bigger boxes.

lesson: Domains and kingdoms
mc1: Which domain contains every organism whose cells have a nucleus? | Eukarya | Bacteria | Archaea | Protista
hint: The name of this domain comes from Greek words meaning "true kernel."
mc1: Which two domains are made of single-celled organisms with no nucleus? | Bacteria and Archaea | Archaea and Eukarya | Bacteria and Eukarya | Eukarya and Protista
hint: Organisms without a nucleus are called prokaryotes. Which domains hold only prokaryotes?
mc1: A mushroom absorbs food from a dead log. Its cell walls contain chitin. Which kingdom is it in? | Fungi | Plantae | Animalia | Protista
hint: It cannot make its own food from sunlight the way a plant does.
mc1: Which kingdom is made of many-celled organisms that make their own food by photosynthesis? | Plantae | Fungi | Animalia | Archaea
hint: Think of moss, ferns and oak trees.
mc2: An amoeba is a single cell with a nucleus. It is not a plant, an animal or a fungus. In the six-kingdom system, where is it placed? | Protista | Bacteria | Archaea | Fungi
hint: Bacteria and archaea have no nucleus, so it cannot belong to either of their kingdoms.
mc2: Which trait do all organisms in kingdom Animalia share? | they are many-celled and get food by consuming other organisms | they all have a backbone | they can all move from place to place their whole lives | they are all single-celled with a nucleus
hint: A sponge is an animal. It has no backbone and stays in one place as an adult.

lesson: Using a dichotomous key
mc2: This key sorts four land animals. Step 1: Does it have feathers? Yes: bird. No: go to step 2. Step 2: Does it have fur or hair? Yes: mammal. No: go to step 3. Step 3: Does it have dry scales? Yes: reptile. No: amphibian. An animal has no feathers, no fur and smooth, moist skin. What is it? | amphibian | reptile | mammal | bird
hint: Answer each step's question in order. Stop when the key names a group.
mc2: A leaf key: 1a. Leaf is a needle, go to 2. 1b. Leaf is broad and flat, go to 3. 2a. Needles grow in bundles: pine. 2b. Needles grow one at a time: spruce. 3a. Leaf edge has teeth: elm. 3b. Leaf edge is smooth: magnolia. A leaf is broad and flat with a smooth edge. Which tree is it from? | magnolia | elm | pine | spruce
hint: Choose 1a or 1b first. That tells you which step to read next.
mc2: This key sorts an insect, a spider, a centipede and a millipede. 1a. Six legs: insect. 1b. Not six legs, go to 2. 2a. Eight legs: spider. 2b. Many more than eight legs, go to 3. 3a. One pair of legs on most body segments: centipede. 3b. Two pairs of legs on most body segments: millipede. An animal has dozens of legs, with two pairs on most segments. What is it? | millipede | centipede | spider | insect
hint: Dozens of legs sends you past step 2. Then look closely at the segments.
mc2: Why does each step of a dichotomous key give exactly two choices? | so every organism fits one choice and the user is never stuck between options | so the key can only be used for two organisms | because there are only two kingdoms | so the user can skip steps
hint: "Dichotomous" means divided into two parts. Why would two clear opposite choices help?
mc3: A student writes this key step: "1a. The animal is big. 1b. The animal is fast." What is the problem with this step? | the choices are not opposites, so an animal could fit both or neither | it has only two choices | it uses traits of animals | it should begin with a species name
hint: Think of a cheetah, which is fairly big and very fast. Which choice would you pick?
sa2: Write one yes-or-no question that separates a crow from a rabbit, a goldfish and a frog. | Does it have feathers? If yes, it is the crow; if no, it is one of the other three animals.

lesson: Vertebrates and invertebrates
mc1: What is the difference between vertebrates and invertebrates? | vertebrates have a backbone and invertebrates do not | vertebrates live on land and invertebrates live in water | vertebrates are large and invertebrates are small | vertebrates are animals and invertebrates are plants
hint: The word "vertebra" names one of the bones in your spine.
mc2: Which animal is an invertebrate? | octopus | shark | snake | frog
hint: Which of these animals has a soft body with no spine at all?
mc2: A shark's skeleton is made of cartilage, not bone. How is a shark classified? | as a vertebrate fish | as an invertebrate, because it has no bones | as a mammal | as an amphibian
hint: A shark still has a spine made of vertebrae; they are just made of cartilage instead of bone.
mc2: About what share of known animal species are invertebrates? | more than 90 percent | about half | about 10 percent | about 25 percent
hint: Think of how many kinds of insects, worms, snails and spiders there are compared with kinds of mammals and birds.
mc2: Which pair are both vertebrates? | bat and salmon | crab and lizard | spider and owl | earthworm and toad
hint: Check each animal in a pair. One invertebrate makes the whole pair wrong.
mc2: A student says a whale is a fish because it lives in water and has fins. What is the best correction? | whales breathe air with lungs and feed their young milk, so they are mammals | whales are invertebrates, so they cannot be fish | whales are amphibians because they live in water | whales are reptiles because they have flippers
hint: Where an animal lives does not decide its group. What do whales do that fish do not?

lesson: Scientific names in action
mc1: Which shows the scientific name of the house cat written correctly? (Printed names are in italics; handwritten ones are underlined.) | Felis catus | felis catus | Felis Catus | Catus felis
hint: The genus comes first and starts with a capital letter. The species name comes second and is all lowercase.
mc2: The gray wolf is Canis lupus and the coyote is Canis latrans. What do these names tell you? | they are in the same genus, so they are closely related | they are the same species | they are in different families | they are not related, because their second names differ
hint: Look at the first word of each name.
mc2: A lion is Panthera leo, a tiger is Panthera tigris and a house cat is Felis catus. Which two are most closely related? | lion and tiger | lion and house cat | tiger and house cat | all three are equally related
hint: Organisms that share a genus share more levels of classification.
mc2: Why do scientists use two-part scientific names instead of common names? | each species gets one name used worldwide, while common names change by language and region | scientific names are always shorter | common names are not allowed in any science writing | scientific names always describe the organism's color
hint: One plant may have five different common names in five countries.
mc3: Scientists find a new beetle. It shares many traits with beetles in the genus Carabus but differs from every known species. How should it be named? | Carabus followed by a new lowercase species name | a new genus name followed by carabus | Carabus with no species name | only an English common name
hint: Which part of the name shows its close relatives, and which part must be new?
sa3: A sea animal has many cells, each with a nucleus. It has no backbone, a soft body and eight arms, and it eats crabs. Kingdom Animalia, because it is many-celled and eats other organisms. Invertebrate, because it has no backbone. (It is an octopus.)

unit: Organic chemistry: functional groups | 13 | sci_13_n1

lesson: Hydrocarbons and their names
mc1: What is a hydrocarbon? | a compound made only of carbon and hydrogen | any compound that contains carbon | a compound of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen | a compound that contains water
hint: Read the word in two parts: hydro- and carbon.
num1: A straight-chain alkane has 5 carbon atoms. How many hydrogen atoms does it have? | 12
hint: Alkanes follow the formula CnH(2n + 2).
mc1: What is the name of the alkane C3H8? | propane | ethane | butane | propene
hint: Count the carbons and match the prefix; the -ane ending means only single bonds.
mc1: Which formula is an alkene? | C2H4 | C2H6 | CH4 | C3H8
hint: An alkene has one C=C double bond, so it follows CnH2n.
mc1: How do ethane and ethene differ? | ethene has a carbon-carbon double bond and two fewer hydrogen atoms | ethene has one more carbon atom | ethane has a carbon-carbon triple bond | ethene contains an oxygen atom
hint: Compare the endings -ane and -ene.
num1: How many carbon atoms are in one molecule of hexane? | 6
hint: The prefix comes from the Greek number word, as in hexagon.

lesson: Spotting functional groups
mc1: Which functional group does CH3CH2OH contain? | alcohol | ketone | carboxylic acid | ester
hint: Look at the group on the end: an O bonded to an H, with no C=O nearby.
mc1: Which functional group does CH3COOH, the acid in vinegar, contain? | carboxylic acid | alcohol | ketone | aldehyde
hint: Find the carbon that is bonded both to a double-bonded O and to an OH.
mc1: In CH3COCH3, a C=O sits between two carbon atoms. Which functional group is this? | ketone | aldehyde | ester | carboxylic acid
hint: Is the C=O at the end of the chain or in the middle?
mc1: Which of these functional groups contains a nitrogen atom? | amine | ester | aldehyde | ketone
hint: This group is related to ammonia, NH3.
mc1: Which functional group does CH3COOCH2CH3 contain? | ester | carboxylic acid | ether | ketone
hint: Compare -COO- here with -COOH. What has replaced the H?
mc2: Where is the C=O group of an aldehyde found? | at the end of a carbon chain, bonded to a hydrogen | in the middle of a chain, between two carbons | on a carbon that also holds an -OH | on a carbon bonded to a nitrogen
hint: Formaldehyde is HCHO. What is attached to its C=O carbon?

lesson: Naming simple organic compounds
mc2: What is the IUPAC name of CH3OH? | methanol | methane | ethanol | methanal
hint: Start with the one-carbon alkane, then change its final -e to the ending used for alcohols.
mc2: What is the IUPAC name of HCHO, commonly called formaldehyde? | methanal | methanol | methanone | ethanal
hint: Aldehyde names end in -al. How many carbons does HCHO have?
mc2: CH3CH2COOH has three carbon atoms, counting the carbon in COOH. What is its IUPAC name? | propanoic acid | propanol | ethanoic acid | propanal
hint: The COOH carbon counts as part of the chain, and acids end in -oic acid.
mc2: What is the IUPAC name of acetone, CH3COCH3? | propanone | propanal | propanol | ethanone
hint: Ketone names end in -one. Count all the carbons, including the C=O carbon.
mc2: What is the IUPAC name of CH3CH2NH2? | ethanamine | ethanol | ethanamide | methanamine
hint: Count the carbons, then add the ending for an -NH2 group. An amide would need a C=O.
mc3: An ester forms when a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol. Which pair forms ethyl ethanoate, CH3COOCH2CH3? | ethanoic acid and ethanol | methanoic acid and propanol | ethanol and ethanal | ethanoic acid and methanol
hint: In an ester name, the first word comes from the alcohol and the "-oate" word comes from the acid.

lesson: Polarity, boiling points and solubility
mc2: Ethanol (C2H5OH, 46 g/mol) boils at about 78 C, but propane (C3H8, 44 g/mol) boils at about -42 C. What best explains the difference? | ethanol molecules form hydrogen bonds through their -OH groups | ethanol molecules have more carbon atoms | propane molecules form stronger hydrogen bonds | ethanol molecules are nonpolar
hint: The masses are almost equal, so look at which molecule has an H bonded to O.
mc2: Which compound is most soluble in water? | methanol | hexane | octane | butane
hint: Water is polar. "Like dissolves like."
mc2: Which molecule is nonpolar? | hexane | ethanol | acetic acid | methylamine
hint: Look for the molecule with no O or N atom at all.
mc2: Which of these three-carbon compounds has the highest boiling point? | propanoic acid | propanal | propane | propanone
hint: Which group can form two hydrogen bonds between pairs of molecules?
mc2: Why does a solution of an amine in water turn slightly basic? | the lone pair on nitrogen accepts an H+ ion from water | the amine releases H+ ions into the water | amines contain -OH groups | the carbon chain reacts with water
hint: A base accepts H+. What does the nitrogen atom have that can bond to H+?
mc2: A student says CH3CH2CHO and CH3COCH3 are the same compound because both have the formula C3H6O. What is the best response? | they are isomers with different functional groups: one is an aldehyde and one is a ketone | they are the same compound written two ways | both of them are alcohols | they have different molecular formulas
hint: Same formula does not mean same structure. Where is the C=O in each?

lesson: Functional groups in the real world
mc3: A chemist heats butanoic acid with ethanol and a little acid catalyst and notices a fruity smell. Which functional group most likely formed? | ester | ketone | amine | ether
hint: What kind of compound forms when an acid reacts with an alcohol?
mc3: An unknown compound turns blue litmus paper red and fizzes when added to sodium bicarbonate, releasing CO2. Which functional group does it most likely contain? | carboxylic acid | alcohol | aldehyde | ester
hint: Which group in the list is acidic enough to react with a weak base?
mc3: Ethanol, a primary alcohol, is oxidized gently. What product forms first? | ethanal, an aldehyde | propanone, a ketone | ethane, an alkane | ethyl ethanoate, an ester
hint: Oxidizing a primary alcohol turns its -CH2OH carbon into a C=O at the end of the chain.
mc3: 2-propanol, a secondary alcohol, is oxidized. What product forms? | propanone | propanal | propanoic acid | propene
hint: In a secondary alcohol, the -OH carbon is in the middle of the chain. Where will the C=O be?
mc3: In a medicine molecule, a chemist finds a nitrogen atom bonded to two carbon atoms and one hydrogen atom, with no C=O next to it. What kind of group is this? | a secondary amine | a primary amine | a tertiary amine | an amide
hint: Count how many carbon atoms are bonded directly to the nitrogen.
sa3: Explain why acetic acid (CH3COOH) mixes easily with water but hexane (C6H14) does not. Use polarity and functional groups. | Acetic acid has a polar carboxylic acid group (-COOH) that forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules, so it dissolves. Hexane is a hydrocarbon with only C-H and C-C bonds, so it is nonpolar and cannot form hydrogen bonds with water; like dissolves like, so it does not mix.
