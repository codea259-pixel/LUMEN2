passage: The Tragedy of Oedipus | Sophocles' Oedipus Rex, first performed in Athens in the fifth century BCE, is often considered the model of Greek tragedy. Its audiences already knew the myth on which it is based, so the power of the play lies not in surprise but in watching the hero discover what they already know. // Before the play begins, Oedipus has become king of Thebes by solving the riddle of the Sphinx, a monster that had terrorized the city. He married the widowed queen, Jocasta. Years earlier, a prophecy had warned that Oedipus would kill his father and marry his mother. Believing that the king and queen of Corinth were his parents, Oedipus fled Corinth to escape this fate. // As the play opens, a plague strikes Thebes. An oracle reveals that the plague will end only when the murderer of the former king, Laius, is found and punished. Oedipus, proud and determined, vows to find the killer. He questions the blind prophet Tiresias, who tells him that he himself is the murderer. Oedipus angrily accuses Tiresias of plotting against him. // Step by step, through the testimony of a messenger and an old shepherd, the truth emerges. Oedipus was the son of Laius and Jocasta; as a baby he had been left to die but was saved and raised in Corinth. On his journey from Corinth, he had killed a stranger in a quarrel on the road, and that stranger was Laius. Jocasta, realizing the truth, takes her own life. Oedipus blinds himself and goes into exile. // Throughout the play, the chorus of Theban elders responds to each discovery, voicing the community's fear and pity. At the end, the chorus warns that no one should be called happy until he has reached the end of his life free of pain. The man who once saw clearly enough to solve the Sphinx's riddle could not see the truth about himself.
