# Grade 3 Science (California NGSS), format 2. Units are original and grouped by topic for classes using Mystery Science.
@subject sci
@grade 3
@program Mystery Science
about: Third graders investigate weather patterns and climates around the world, design solutions to weather hazards, explore balanced and unbalanced forces, magnets and static electricity, model life cycles and inherited traits, and argue from evidence about how organisms survive, live in groups and change over time. They also practice the engineering design process.
std: 3-PS2-1 | Plan and conduct an investigation to provide evidence of the effects of balanced and unbalanced forces on the motion of an object.
std: 3-PS2-3 | Ask questions to determine cause and effect relationships of electric or magnetic interactions between two objects not in contact with each other.
std: 3-LS1-1 | Develop models to describe that organisms have unique and diverse life cycles but all have in common birth, growth, reproduction and death.
std: 3-LS3-1 | Analyze and interpret data to provide evidence that plants and animals have traits inherited from parents and that variation of these traits exists in a group of similar organisms.
std: 3-LS4-3 | Construct an argument with evidence that in a particular habitat some organisms can survive well, some survive less well and some cannot survive at all.
std: 3-ESS2-1 | Represent data in tables and graphical displays to describe typical weather conditions expected during a particular season.
std: 3-ESS3-1 | Make a claim about the merit of a design solution that reduces the impacts of a weather-related hazard.
std: 3-5-ETS1-2 | Generate and compare multiple possible solutions to a problem based on how well each is likely to meet the criteria and constraints of the problem.

Evidence: Frogs dropped from 120 to 30 and ducks dropped from 45 to 20, but field mice rose from 15 to 40. Reasoning: Frogs and ducks need water to live, find food and reproduce, so they survived less well when the water was removed.
