about: Fourth grade readers refer to details and examples when explaining what a text says and what it suggests, determine themes and main ideas, describe characters in depth, explain how authors use reasons and evidence, and compare firsthand and secondhand accounts. Writers produce organized opinion, informative and narrative pieces, and build vocabulary through Greek and Latin roots, affixes and figurative language. The 10 units below follow a year of knowledge topics; teachers can match them to their pacing guide.
std: RL.4.1 | Refer to details and examples in a text when explaining what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences from the text.
std: RL.4.2 | Determine a theme of a story, drama or poem from details in the text; summarize the text.
std: RL.4.3 | Describe in depth a character, setting or event in a story or drama, drawing on specific details in the text.
std: RI.4.2 | Determine the main idea of a text and explain how it is supported by key details; summarize the text.
std: RI.4.8 | Explain how an author uses reasons and evidence to support particular points in a text.
std: W.4.1 | Write opinion pieces on topics or texts, supporting a point of view with reasons and information.
std: W.4.2 | Write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas and information clearly.
std: L.4.4 | Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown words using context, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and reference materials.
