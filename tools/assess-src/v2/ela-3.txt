about: Third grade marks the shift from learning to read to reading to learn. Students decode multisyllable words with prefixes and suffixes, recount stories and explain how key details convey the central message, describe characters and how their actions shape events, find main ideas and connections in informational texts, and write opinion, informative and narrative pieces in paragraphs. Ten knowledge units (about three weeks each) pair a science or social studies topic with a reading focus and a writing focus, plus grammar, conventions and vocabulary.
std: RF.3.3 | Know and apply grade-level phonics and word analysis skills, including common prefixes and suffixes, Latin suffixes and multisyllable words.
std: RL.3.2 | Recount stories, including fables, folktales and myths from diverse cultures; determine the central message, lesson or moral and explain how it is conveyed through key details.
std: RL.3.3 | Describe characters in a story (their traits, motivations or feelings) and explain how their actions contribute to the sequence of events.
std: RI.3.2 | Determine the main idea of a text; recount the key details and explain how they support the main idea.
std: RI.3.3 | Describe the relationship between a series of historical events, scientific ideas or steps, using language about time, sequence and cause and effect.
std: W.3.1 | Write opinion pieces on topics or texts, supporting a point of view with reasons, linking words and a concluding statement.
std: W.3.2 | Write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas and information clearly, with facts, definitions and details.
std: L.3.1 | Demonstrate command of grade 3 grammar and usage, including abstract nouns, verb tenses, subject-verb agreement, comparatives and superlatives, conjunctions and compound and complex sentences.
