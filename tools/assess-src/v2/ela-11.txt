passage: Two Voices of Self-Reliance | In the 1840s and 1850s, Ralph Waldo Emerson and Henry David Thoreau, neighbors in Concord, Massachusetts, became the best-known voices of Transcendentalism. Both urged Americans to trust their own minds, look to nature for truth and resist the pressure to conform. // In his essay "Self-Reliance" (1841), Emerson wrote: "Trust thyself: every heart vibrates to that iron string." Later in the same essay he declared: "Whoso would be a man, must be a nonconformist." // From 1845 to 1847, Thoreau lived in a small cabin he built near Walden Pond, on land owned by Emerson. In Walden (1854), he explained his reasons: "I went to the woods because I wished to live deliberately, to front only the essential facts of life, and see if I could not learn what it had to teach, and not, when I came to die, discover that I had not lived." He also warned against the clutter of modern life: "Our life is frittered away by detail. An honest man has hardly need to count more than his ten fingers, or in extreme cases he may add his ten toes, and lump the rest. Simplicity, simplicity, simplicity!" // Emerson spoke mostly in bold general principles; Thoreau tested those principles in a personal experiment. Together, their writing helped shape a lasting American belief in individual conscience and the value of the natural world.

passage: The Universe Replies | In the late 1800s, many American writers rejected the idealism of earlier Romantic writers. Realists such as Mark Twain and William Dean Howells described ordinary life as truthfully as they could. Naturalists such as Stephen Crane, Jack London and Frank Norris went further, suggesting that humans are shaped by forces they cannot control, such as heredity, economic conditions and the power of nature. Stephen Crane expressed this view in a short poem published in 1899: // A man said to the universe: / "Sir, I exist!" / "However," replied the universe, / "The fact has not created in me / A sense of obligation." // - Stephen Crane (1899) // In just five lines, Crane captures a central Naturalist idea. The man speaks boldly, as if his existence should matter to the cosmos. The universe answers politely, even formally, with "Sir" echoed in its calm "However," but its message is cold: it owes the man nothing. Unlike the Transcendentalists, who saw nature as a source of spiritual truth and connection, Crane presents a universe that is vast, impersonal and indifferent to human hopes.

mc2: According to "The Universe Replies," how did Naturalists differ from Realists? | Naturalists emphasized forces such as heredity, economics and nature that humans cannot control. | Naturalists wrote only about nature walks. | Naturalists idealized heroes. | Naturalists rejected describing ordinary life.
mc3: In "The Universe Replies," how does Crane's view of nature contrast with the Transcendentalists' view? | Crane sees nature as impersonal and indifferent, while Transcendentalists saw it as a source of spiritual truth. | Both saw nature as cruel and evil. | Crane worshipped nature, while Transcendentalists feared it. | Neither wrote about nature.
mc2: In "The Universe Replies," what does the man's exclamation "Sir, I exist!" suggest about him? | He believes his existence should be important to the universe. | He is unsure whether he exists. | He is angry at another person. | He is giving a scientific lecture.
mc2: In "The Universe Replies," why does the author describe the universe as answering "politely, even formally"? | to highlight the contrast between its manners and its indifferent message | to show the universe respects the man deeply | to suggest the poem is humorous only | to describe Crane's personality
sa3: Using "The Universe Replies," explain how Crane's poem reflects Naturalism. Cite the poem. | The universe replies that the man's existence "has not created in me / A sense of obligation," showing that nature is indifferent to human beings. This matches the Naturalist belief that humans are small and shaped by forces beyond their control, not watched over by a caring universe.
