# Grade 5 Science (California NGSS), format 2. Units are original and grouped by topic for classes using Mystery Science.

@program Mystery Science
about: Fifth graders build a particle model of matter, identify materials by their properties, and investigate whether mixing substances makes new substances while total mass stays the same. They trace how plants get the materials they need from air and water, how energy from the Sun moves through food webs and how matter cycles through ecosystems. They model how Earth's systems interact, describe how water is distributed, explore ways communities protect resources, and analyze patterns of gravity, shadows, day and night and the stars, while applying the engineering design process.
std: 5-PS1-1 | Develop a model to describe that matter is made of particles too small to be seen.
std: 5-PS1-2 | Measure and graph quantities to provide evidence that regardless of the type of change that occurs when heating, cooling or mixing substances, the total weight of matter is conserved.
std: 5-PS1-4 | Conduct an investigation to determine whether the mixing of two or more substances results in new substances.
std: 5-LS1-1 | Support an argument that plants get the materials they need for growth chiefly from air and water.
std: 5-LS2-1 | Develop a model to describe the movement of matter among plants, animals, decomposers and the environment.
std: 5-ESS2-1 | Develop a model using an example to describe ways the geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere and/or atmosphere interact.
std: 5-ESS1-2 | Represent data in graphical displays to reveal patterns of daily changes in length and direction of shadows, day and night, and the seasonal appearance of some stars in the night sky.
std: 3-5-ETS1-3 | Plan and carry out fair tests in which variables are controlled and failure points are considered to identify aspects of a model or prototype that can be improved.

Orbit is Earth traveling around the Sun about once every 365 days; it causes different stars to be visible in different seasons.
cer3: Students measured the shadow of a 1-meter stick on the same day: 8 a.m. 250 cm pointing west; 10 a.m. 110 cm pointing northwest; 12 p.m. 70 cm pointing north; 2 p.m. 110 cm pointing northeast; 4 p.m. 250 cm pointing east. Make a claim about what causes the shadow to change during the day. Use evidence from the data and explain your reasoning. | Claim: Earth's rotation changes the Sun's position in our sky, which changes the length and direction of shadows. Evidence: The shadow was long (250 cm) and pointed west in the morning, shortest (70 cm) and pointed north at noon, and long again (250 cm) pointing east in the afternoon. Reasoning: As Earth rotates, the Sun appears to rise in the east, climb high at midday and set in the west. Shadows point away from the Sun and are shortest when the Sun is highest, so the shadow swings from west to east and is shortest at noon.
