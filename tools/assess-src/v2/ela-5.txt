about: Fifth grade readers quote accurately from a text when explaining what it says and what it suggests, determine themes from how characters respond to challenges, compare characters and texts, explain how structure and point of view shape meaning, and integrate information from several sources. Writers produce logically organized opinion, informative and narrative pieces, and build vocabulary through Greek and Latin roots and affixes, figurative language, idioms and word relationships. The 10 units below follow a year of knowledge topics; teachers can match them to their pacing guide.
std: RL.5.1 | Quote accurately from a text when explaining what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences from the text.
std: RL.5.2 | Determine a theme of a story, drama or poem from details in the text, including how characters respond to challenges; summarize the text.
std: RL.5.3 | Compare and contrast two or more characters, settings or events in a story or drama, drawing on specific details in the text.
std: RI.5.2 | Determine two or more main ideas of a text and explain how they are supported by key details; summarize the text.
std: RI.5.5 | Compare and contrast the overall structure of events, ideas, concepts or information in two or more texts.
std: RI.5.8 | Explain how an author uses reasons and evidence to support particular points in a text.
std: W.5.1 | Write opinion pieces on topics or texts, supporting a point of view with reasons and information.
std: L.5.4 | Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown words using context, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and reference materials.

"You keep thinking, 'Five hundred yards. Five hundred yards.' That's too big to swim. Count your strokes instead. One, two, three, breathe. Just swim the stroke you're on." // Malia rolled her eyes.
