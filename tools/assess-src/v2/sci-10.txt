cer3: A class heated wooden splints soaked in different salt solutions in a burner flame while wearing goggles. Sodium chloride and sodium nitrate both gave a yellow-orange flame. Potassium chloride and potassium nitrate both gave a lilac flame. Make a claim about which part of each salt causes the flame color. Use the data as evidence and explain your reasoning with electron energy levels. | Claim: the metal (sodium or potassium) causes the flame color, not the chloride or nitrate part. Evidence: both sodium salts gave yellow-orange even though one was a chloride and one a nitrate, and both potassium salts gave lilac. Changing the nonmetal part did not change the color, but changing the metal did. Reasoning: heat excites electrons in the metal atoms; when they fall back to lower energy levels they release light with energies set by that element's energy levels, so each metal gives its own color.
